WHY IT MATTERS

A missing gene label can send research in the wrong direction.

A gene may really differ between organisms. It may also look different because the DNA was read, reconstructed or labeled imperfectly. Researchers need to tell these possibilities apart before planning follow-up experiments.

GeneTrace connects the checks. It brings biological context and computational evidence into one investigation, shows uncertainty, and helps researchers choose what to verify next.

  1. 01Reads

    DNA sequence fragments in a sequencing dataset.

  2. 02Assembly

    A reconstructed genome, built from those fragments.

  3. 03Annotation

    Labels marking gene locations and structure.

Think of reconstructing a book: reads are snippets, assembly puts the text together, and annotation marks the chapters. A missing chapter label does not prove the text is gone. This is an analogy, not a one-to-one biological mapping.

New to genomics? A quick guide to the terms
Genome · gene · protein
A genome is an organism's DNA. A gene is a DNA region that contributes to a functional RNA or protein product. Proteins perform many cellular tasks; not every gene makes a protein.
Strain · reference · 1:N comparison
A strain is a distinct lineage within a microbial species. A reference is a comparison starting point, not an automatic truth. A 1:N comparison checks one genome against many related genomes.
Allele · natural variation
An allele is an alternative DNA sequence at a location. A rare or minority allele can be real biology; it is not automatically an error.
Contig
A continuous stretch of reconstructed DNA. An assembly may contain many contigs instead of complete chromosomes.
CDS · structural annotation
A CDS is an annotated protein-coding region. Structural annotation describes gene locations and organization; it does not by itself prove a functional protein.
Depth · mismatch
Depth counts how many aligned reads cover a position. A mismatch is a base difference between a read and the assembly. Higher depth or a mismatch alone does not establish accuracy or cause.
Indel · frameshift
An indel is an insertion or deletion relative to another sequence. In a coding region, an indel whose length is not a multiple of three can shift how bases are grouped for translation. Indels may be natural variation or data problems.
Sequence identity · query coverage
Identity measures matching positions within an alignment. Query coverage describes how much of the searched sequence is covered by that alignment. A highly similar short match may not represent a full gene.
Synteny · flanking genes
Synteny describes conserved gene order or location. Genes on either side help check whether two matches belong to corresponding genomic neighborhoods.
Homolog group · orthogroup · protein domain
Homologs are related by common ancestry. An orthogroup traces genes to one ancestral gene in the last common ancestor of the compared species. A protein domain is a recognizable region within a protein. Similarity clusters or shared domains alone do not establish orthology or identical function.
Provenance · contamination · FCS
Provenance is where data came from and how it was processed. Contamination is sequence from an unintended source. NCBI FCS is an external contamination screen used here for comparison; its labels do not establish every kind of sequencing or assembly error.

How Meta powers the investigation

Meta Model API · muse-spark-1.3 is the reasoning planner inside our custom GeneTrace agent.

  1. Read the state: the model receives the research question, current evidence and currently allowed checks.
  2. Select a check: it returns a structured tool choice, selection reason and hypotheses with evidence IDs.
  3. Execute and reassess: GeneTrace validates that choice, runs the fixed analysis code and sends the new evidence back to the model.
  4. Explain the result: the model synthesizes provisional interpretations and limitations with evidence citations.

The verified nimT execution used four planning responses and one final synthesis to execute four fresh checks. Commands and tool inputs are fixed by code. Required-check rules and the structured judgment are deterministic; this is a bounded agent, not unrestricted model autonomy.
